Abstract

Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) requires real-world agents to understand natural-language instructions and align them with continuous visual inputs to navigate in unseen environments. Despite strong visual-semantic capabilities, VLM-based navigation agents require intensive training data to generalize across diverse scenes and complex 3D interactions. Moreover, existing observation-to-action agents overlook the rich information available along navigation trajectories.

We propose FINE, a future-informed navigation encoding framework for learning effective VLN from fewer demonstrations by exploiting future trajectory information during training. FINE incorporates two complementary representations: explicit landmark-level semantics and geometry, and implicit scene-level evolution and navigation progress. Together, these objectives provide richer navigation-relevant supervision from each trajectory without requiring future observations at inference.

Experiments on continuous VLN benchmarks R2R-CE and RxR-CE, together with real-world humanoid robot navigation, show that FINE consistently improves navigation performance, especially under limited training data, enabling models trained on fewer demonstrations to approach full-data performance.

Index Terms—Vision-Based Navigation, Deep Learning for Visual Perception, Representation Learning, Imitation Learning, AI-Enabled Robotics

Why FINE?

Standard VLM-based VLN training primarily maps observations and navigation history directly to actions or trajectories. This action-centric supervision underuses the structure already present in a trajectory: which landmarks will become important, how scene geometry changes, and how the agent progresses toward the goal.

This limitation is especially important in the low-data regime, where limited scene and route coverage makes it difficult to separate navigation-critical cues from incidental visual details. FINE extracts more supervision from each demonstration by learning future-aware representations at complementary landmark and navigation-state levels.

Across continuous VLN benchmarks and real-world robot navigation, FINE is designed to improve navigation success and path efficiency while allowing reduced-data models to approach the performance of substantially larger training sets.

Overview

FINE learns from future trajectory information during training to build explicit landmark-level and implicit scene-level navigation representations, without requiring future observations at inference.

Explicit Landmark Modeling

The explicit branch uses future observations during training to identify instruction-relevant landmarks and encode their semantic and geometric structure into dedicated landmark tokens. These tokens provide navigation-critical semantic-spatial anchors instead of treating all visible content equally.

Implicit Navigation-State Modeling

In parallel, the implicit branch summarizes predictive scene-level context from future navigation states, capturing latent world evolution and route progression beyond individual objects. This complements explicit landmark grounding with broader information about where the agent is heading.
